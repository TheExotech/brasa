Burning Records Across Satellites, Aligned

One fire record from 2000 to today, whatever satellite saw it.

MODIS (Terra 2000–, Aqua 2002–) built the longest active-fire record, but it is ending. VIIRS (S-NPP 2012–, NOAA-20/21) carries it forward with 375 m pixels that detect several times more hotspots. Splicing the two creates a fake jump in fire activity. BRASA removes that jump so the calendar shows real patterns, trends and anomalies.

1 · Why the raw records cannot be compared

  • Pixel size. A MODIS pixel is ~1 km at nadir (up to ~2×4.8 km at the scan edge); a VIIRS I-band pixel is 375 m (~7× smaller area). One fire front often lights 1 MODIS pixel but 3–8 VIIRS pixels (Schroeder et al., 2014).
  • Sensitivity. VIIRS detects many small/cool agricultural and grassland fires that MODIS misses (Fu et al., 2020).
  • Overpass time. Aqua and S-NPP cross at ~13:30/01:30; Terra at ~10:30/22:30 samples a different part of the diurnal fire cycle (Giglio et al., 2006).
  • Orbit drift. Aqua began drifting later in July 2021 and Terra earlier since 2020; both reach end of mission in 2026–27.
  • Published raw VIIRS/MODIS ratios range 2.8–11×, ~5× for South America (White, 2023), and vary by region, season and day/night, so no single global factor works.

2 · The harmonization algorithm

  1. Filter. FIRMS Standard Processing records only (NRT only after the last SP date). Keep type = 0 (presumed vegetation fire), excluding volcanoes, static industrial sources and offshore flares. MODIS confidence ≥ 30; VIIRS confidence nominal/high. NRT records inherit static-source flags from the SP record at ~1 km.
  2. Footprint-equivalent deduplication. Every detection is snapped to a 0.01° (~1 km) cell; one count per cell × day × day/night × platform (an active-fire cell-day). This removes the 375 m-vs-1 km pixel multiplicity, bow-tie duplicates and repeat looks from overlapping orbits.
  3. Reference satellite. As INPE does for Brazil's official hotspot series, the record is expressed in Aqua-MODIS-equivalent units: Aqua shares the 13:30 orbit with VIIRS and spans 2002–2026. Users can switch to VIIRS-equivalent units.
  4. Calibration on the stable overlap (2012-02 to 2021-06, before Aqua drift; months where either sensor has an outage are excluded):
    E[Aqua] = rdn(month) · θcell,dn · VIIRS
    • rdn: regional ratio, fitted separately for day and night;
    • rdn(month): seasonal ratio, shrunk toward the annual ratio with 50 pseudo-counts;
    • θ: per-0.5° cell multiplier with empirical-Bayes (Poisson–gamma) shrinkage (Clayton & Kaldor, 1987; Marshall, 1991): data-rich cells keep their own ratio, sparse cells borrow from the region.
    Terra → Aqua is calibrated the same way on 2003–2019 to extend the record back to Nov 2000.
  5. Assembly. Terra→Aqua-eq (2000-11 … 2002-06) · Aqua observed (2002-07 … 2021-06) · VIIRS→Aqua-eq (2021-07 … today). If S-NPP has an outage in a month, observed Aqua is used and the month is flagged.
  6. Uncertainty. 90 % prediction intervals for every converted month come from the empirical distribution of leave-one-year-out residuals, binned by count size (so small AOIs get honestly wider intervals).
  7. Anomalies. Per calendar month, the median of a user-selected baseline (default 2003–2022, the target year left out), percentile rank, robust z-score and log2 ratio. Months where both value and median are < 3 are not coloured as anomalies.

Three things that went wrong before this page existed

The grid was 0.25° first. Most cells in Argentina hold a few counts a month at that size, the shrinkage pulls them all back to the regional ratio and the cell term stops doing work. 0.5° is the coarsest grid where 2,164 cells still carry signal.

We called 2020 an artefact. It is the biggest year in the raw series and it sits where the swath geometry shifts, so it looked like the 2012 break. Cross-validation said otherwise: 2020 and 2021 hold at the top of the harmonized series, and the break is 2012 (raw ×3.7 in a year, Aqua flat). The chart is re-annotated.

One global ratio nearly won. N1 scores better than the adopted model per cell (MAE 0.436 vs 0.444). It loses at region-month, which is what the app reports, but it stays in the table because a reader deserves to see how close the cheap model gets.

3 · Validation

Leave-one-year-out cross-validation over the overlap: each year is predicted from VIIRS with a model fitted on the other years, then compared with the Aqua observation. All models are scored on the same deduplicated Aqua target, at region-month and 0.5°-cell-month level.

bias = Σpred/Σobs − 1 · MAE and R² on log(1+count) · Spearman ρ of the monthly series. N0 splices raw VIIRS without correction: the bias it shows is the artificial jump harmonization removes.

Both levels are in the tables above on purpose. At 0.5° cell-month the adopted model is not the best row: N1 beats it there (MAE 0.436, R² 0.761). M3 wins at region-month, which is the level the calendar reports, and the wide intervals on small AOIs are the price of that choice. Anyone working cell by cell should read N1 as the stronger model.

Aqua orbit-drift diagnostic

Observed (drifting) Aqua ÷ VIIRS-derived Aqua-equivalent after 2021-07. Year-to-year departures from 1 of up to ±20 % show that the drifting Aqua orbit is no longer a stable reference, which is why BRASA switches to VIIRS for this period.

S-NPP → NOAA-20 continuity

NASA LANCE is moving from S-NPP to NOAA-20/21 (S-NPP NRT delivery stopped on 2 Nov 2026 in our data). BRASA calibrates NOAA-20 to S-NPP on 2024 → present and chains it with S-NPP → Aqua, so the record continues seamlessly.

4 · Limitations

  • Hotspots are fire pixels, not fires or burned area; cloud and smoke can hide fires.
  • Calibration is regional. Very small AOIs inherit regional/cell ratios and have wide intervals (shown in tooltips and CSV).
  • Aggregation uses a 0.1° grid with area-weighted partial cells; hotspot CSV exports are exact to the AOI polygon.
  • Precomputed coverage: Argentina and the Philippines (the pipeline adds any country in one line). The live NRT layer works anywhere.
  • S-NPP is being phased out in favour of NOAA-20/21; the same calibration machinery extends to them (nested ratio).
